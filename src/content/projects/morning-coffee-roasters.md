## The brief

Morning had outgrown its first shop and wanted the second location to put craft on display: the roaster, the barists, the beans. The room needed to work hard at peak hour — forty seats, a queue that coils without blocking the street door — while feeling calm enough to stay for a second cup.

## The concept

We organised the plan around a U-shaped bar that lets customers watch every stage of brewing. Ash panelling lines the walls and continues into the furniture, so tables and benches read as extensions of the architecture. Lighting is layered: high-output track over the bar for work, low pendants over the seating for atmosphere.

- Counter-height communal table along the window for solo visitors
- Custom pour-over stations sized to the client's exact brew bars
- Acoustic felt ceiling panels concealed above open shelving

## The outcome

The café opened to queues in its first week and the communal window table has become the shop's signature — photographed daily, sat-in all day. Foot traffic from the corner junction is up measurably, which the client credits to the facades deep recessed entry.
